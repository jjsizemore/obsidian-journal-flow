> [!info]- Plain-language distortion guide
> These are self-reflection prompts, not diagnoses.
>
> **Catastrophizing** — Treating the worst outcome as though it is likely or inevitable.  
> *Ask:* What outcome is most likely, and how would I respond if the feared outcome occurred?
>
> **All-or-Nothing Thinking** — Seeing only total success or total failure.  
> *Ask:* What is the middle ground or partial success?
>
> **Emotional Reasoning** — Treating a feeling as proof that a conclusion is true.  
> *Ask:* What evidence exists beyond the feeling itself?
>
> **Magnification of the Negative** — Giving a setback more weight than its context supports.  
> *Ask:* How important will this seem later, and what remains intact?
>
> **Minimization of the Positive** — Discounting positive evidence or achievements.  
> *Ask:* Would I dismiss the same evidence if it belonged to someone else?
>
> **Jumping to Conclusions** — Reaching a conclusion before enough evidence is available.  
> *Ask:* What do I know, and what am I assuming?
>
> **Fortune Telling** — Treating a negative prediction as a settled fact.  
> *Ask:* What other outcomes are genuinely possible?
>
> **Mind Reading** — Assuming another person's thoughts or motives without direct evidence.  
> *Ask:* What observable evidence supports this, and what are other explanations?
>
> **Self-blaming** — Assigning yourself excessive responsibility for an outcome.  
> *Ask:* What other people, conditions, or constraints contributed?
>
> **Other-blaming** — Assigning all responsibility elsewhere.  
> *Ask:* What part, however small, is within my control?
>
> **Filtering Out Positive** — Attending only to negative information.  
> *Ask:* What was neutral, useful, or positive that I have not counted?
>
> **Overgeneralizing** — Turning a limited event into a broad rule.  
> *Ask:* What exceptions exist, and how often has this actually happened?
>
> **Labeling** — Turning a behavior or event into a fixed identity judgment.  
> *Ask:* What specific behavior or outcome can I describe without defining the whole person?
>
> **Should / Must Statements** — Using rigid demands where a preference or goal would be more accurate.  
> *Ask:* How would this sound as “I would prefer,” “I value,” or “I can try”?

## 3. How can you challenge the thought?

> [!tip]- Sentence starters
> - Even though...
> - The facts say...
> - It's possible that...
> - If a friend were asking for advice...

Consider what is known versus assumed, evidence that supports or weakens the thought, and what you would say to someone else in the same situation.
